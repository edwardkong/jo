I've kept a notebook on and off since school. Mostly off. Every few years I buy a new one, fill the first twenty pages in a fortnight, and then it sits in a drawer until I find it again and feel vaguely guilty.

This year has been different, and I think the difference is that I stopped trying to write anything good in it.

## What goes in

Almost nothing of consequence. A typical page from last week:

- the name of a film someone mentioned
- a note that the starter needs feeding on Thursday
- half a sentence about a conversation I didn't finish
- a list of things to buy that I never took to the shop

It is not a diary, and it is not a planner. It's closer to a table I leave things on so I can stop carrying them.

## Why it stays there

Most of it will never be read again, including by me. I used to think that made it pointless. Now I think it's the whole function. Writing something down is a way of deciding it's dealt with.

There's a line I copied from somewhere, I no longer know where:

> The notebook is not for remembering. It is for forgetting safely.

Some of it does end up here, rewritten. That's what this site is for: the small fraction that's worth saying out loud, said to nobody in particular.
